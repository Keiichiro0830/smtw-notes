6. 1st meeting summary (April 11, 2026)

Saturday, April 11, 2026 · 10:30–12:15 JST · Waseda University. Pre-read: Bank of Japan Policy Board Member Takata's lecture (PDF, JP). The full Japanese minutes are in the JP portal; an English condensed version follows.

  • Aim: 12 monthly discussions over a year, eventually published as a book.

6.2 Self-introductions & discussion on AI

Iinuma (SBI Shinsei Bank)

  • Teams + Copilot for minutes is now standard at firms. Concern: junior staff skip the "apprenticeship" of writing minutes themselves.
  • AI accuracy ~80%; final human check still needed.
  • Resonates with Dōgen's shikantaza — understanding through doing with one's own hands.

Kuroda (faculty)

  • AI cannot yet produce professional-grade output (papers fall short).
  • But within a few years, all but the top few percent may be displaced.
  • AI literacy gap is widening among students. AI is an amplifier of existing ability.

Sasaki (CPA)

  • Joined because Prof. Kawaguchi's view — "Japanese business may be a cooperative game, not a non-cooperative one" — resonated.

K. Suzuki (Sumitomo Mitsui Trust Research Institute)

  • AI is a leverage on ability. If the base is small, the output stays small.
  • From farming → IT → AI, individual variance grows. Polarization is the baseline trajectory.
  • An era of intellectual forging is exactly what's needed.

Asakawa (German printing-press maker, finance)

  • Copilot use is spreading at his workplace; clear gap between graduate-experienced staff and older employees.
  • Capitalist trajectory: society splits into top tier, AI-using tier, and non-using tier — three layers.

J. Suzuki (Real estate)

  • AI is highly useful as research entry point — pinpoints relevant pages within hundreds of pages of SEC documents.

Miyatake (Waseda Business School M2)

  • Independent value-up consultant ex-PE; building a new business.
  • Started replicating finance papers (J. of Finance, etc.) on Japanese-market data using Claude Code agents — 100 papers/month feasible. Felt the power.

Prof. Kawaguchi's view of AI

  • Herbert Simon framed intelligence as "symbol systems"; the 1980s expert systems failed in practice.
  • Today's generative AI is not a symbol system but a numerical system — chains the most-likely next word via probability.
  • No thought, no intelligence behind it. Humans project cognition and feel as if there is. ChatGPT's brilliance is in exploiting that illusion.
  • AI = "search + monologue." Search and summary are useful.
  • AI bubble: a ¥2,000 Claude writes a report sold for ¥1M–¥10M. The bubble bursts the moment the information asymmetry collapses.

Matsumae's reflection

  • As white-collar expertise gets taken by AI, political instability may follow.
  • The skill of asking good questions matters more.
  • If AI absorbs poor ethics, humans may not be able to push back. The capacity to ask "what is true / good / beautiful" is essential.

6.3 Japanese economy & bubble debate

Prof. Kawaguchi presented BoJ Policy Board Member Takata's lecture. Takata argues "structural change, not bubble": corporate profits surged from ~2004; labor share has not risen; real interest rates are negative and stand out among G7.

Matsumae

Cannot rule out structural change and bubble running in parallel. Real estate also feels too high.

K. Suzuki

Some bubble symptoms, but long-term, equities and real estate likely keep rising — analogous to UK→US hegemonic transition. Short-term: Hiroo Garden Hills listed at ¥800M, sold at ¥600M.

Miyatake

In nominal terms, not a Heisei-style bubble (PER/PBR not at extreme). But real-vs-nominal divergence widens. Low labor share is the structural issue. Some call it a "high-stock recession."

Iinuma

Labor share won't rise easily — managers' mindset is shaped by the 1990 bubble experience. Will take time. Structural change continues alongside this stasis.

Asakawa

Companies that should be culled survive on subsidies. Bad management persists; low labor share is reinforced.

Miyatake (regional PE reality)

Regions are in a vicious cycle. Population shrink → consumption stalls → no investment recovery → market shrinks 1%/yr → no room to raise share. Subsidies just pad the owner's pocket. Painful reform doesn't happen.

Kuroda

Mechanisms differ wildly by sector and group. Need decomposition rather than aggregate indices. Affordability research is underdone in Japan.

Monetary policy & excess liquidity

  • 2020 COVID → global easing → 2022 inflation → US sharp rate hike → late-2024 Trump tariff effects re-trigger easing.
  • Prof. Kawaguchi's hypothesis: excess liquidity is the main driver of equity & real-estate price rises. "This money is not going to retreat."

Real-estate market

  • Tokyo apartments massively up. Power couples need ¥150M–¥200M. Inside the Yamanote line, ¥200M won't suffice.
  • Rents up ~20%/yr (instantaneous). Studio ¥70K → ¥150K.
  • Foreign capital (notably Chinese) buying up buildings at high prices. Mitsubishi Estate's CEO: "I don't see the logic."
  • Three-tier divergence: Tokyo up, Osaka flat, Nagoya down.

Conditions for a bubble crash (Prof. Kawaguchi)

  • Rule of thumb: real estate doesn't fall while equities rise (no historical exception).
  • The crash comes when dry powder runs out. With negative real rates, it doesn't.
  • Triggers: BoJ or FSA forcing banks to tighten. FSA already tightened against regional banks for real-estate lending; megabanks not yet.
  • The wealthy exit early (proved in US tax data around Lehman / dot-com crashes).

Population decline

  • Japan loses population at WWII death-rate levels each year.
  • In 10–20 years, the 47 prefectures may be consolidated to 5–7.
  • Tokyo holds until ~2045; declines thereafter.

Real-estate Security Tokens (ST)

  • Marketed as filling the gap between physical real estate and J-REIT; in reality, a short-duration retail product.
  • Legally a gray zone. J-REIT industry wants ST excluded; FSA prefers ST over private REITs.
  • Appraisal industry is the base; REIT, ST, and physical real estate sectors are at odds.

6.4 Proposal for next time (Matsumae)

  • Real-estate price modeling — interest-rate as the key driver, segmented by money's nature and area.
  • If data permits, build an AI-assisted pricing model.
