Yuichiro Kawaguchi — A Practitioner's Guide to LLMs & Generative AI — Research & Practice: Loop Engineering (Ver2)

The slide deck the professor will use at the 4th meeting (24 slides) — a "provisional synthesis" of the April–June discussions of what LLMs are and how to use them. The first half draws on Ferrara (U. Pittsburgh), NBER WP 35374 — a practitioner's guide to LLMs and generative AI in economic-history research: limitations and risks, the iterative-dialogue workflow, and worked examples (structuring newspaper archives, classifying 630k paintings, and more). The middle section is the loop-engineering exercise part (one-slide digests of the Lynch and Ng pre-reads, how to design "your own loop," and the two questions to prepare before the session); the closing section presents the "LLM as system connector" framing, the "cheap verification" criterion, and the "rigid skeleton, soft edges" design principle. Ver2 (July 18) expands the pre-reads page into two slides — Lynch (p.17) and Ng (p.18). (Japanese)

Loop-engineering exercise — pre-reads (Lynch & Ng)

The session features an exercise in which each participant designs "their own loop," matched to their interests and AI skill level. Two pre-reads: Jess Lynch (founding GP of a pre-seed VC), "10 Ways I Use Claude Code as My Chief of Staff" (with the professor's Japanese translation) — a worked example of putting one's own work, across email, CRM, and documents, onto AI loops; and Andrew Ng's The Batch #359. Read them while recalling one or two things you repeatedly fix or repeatedly re-instruct in your own work, and you will drop straight into the exercise.

Theme document · shared by Prof. Kawaguchi, 2026-07-13
Yuichiro Kawaguchi — How to use LLMs (an example): LLM as system connector, not thinking brain

The professor's framing: the revolution now under way in practice — especially in back offices — is "API → LLM → the democratization of software." In Gemini's phrasing, the LLM is not a "thinking brain" but "flexible glue connecting systems" (e.g., Salesforce's Agentforce Operations). At the same time: given how LLMs actually work, are there in fact few real-estate jobs an LLM could replace? (To be discussed beyond real estate.) The attached DOCX is Claude's answer to the same question — a single criterion ("what decides success is not model intelligence but how cheaply outputs can be verified"), four conditions for good use cases, and the design principle of "rigid skeleton, soft edges." (Japanese)

From the 2025 Kawaguchi seminar · shared 2026-06-21
Yuichiro Kawaguchi — Tutorial on Spatio-Temporal Statistics with R — Session 1 slides

Shared in response to the 3rd-meeting comment that "those who did not take the 2025 seminar can't follow the claim that LLMs are an extension of spatio-temporal statistics." Session 1 slides (59 pages), in two parts: (1) experience running someone else's R code by copy-and-paste — Dogen's shikantaza, learning by doing; (2) memorize the two key pages, p.2 (statistical models) and p.9 (inner products and Gram matrices) — the doorway to seeing an LLM as a kind of regression analysis. The session-1 video (unlisted YouTube) is currently offline; the professor is open to re-publishing on demand if requested. The PPTX opens with the same password as this portal. (Japanese/English)
