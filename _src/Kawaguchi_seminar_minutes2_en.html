7.1 Prof. Kawaguchi: "Generative AI is the new RStudio" — a one-month shift in perception

  • Over the past month Prof. Kawaguchi has used Claude (USD 20/mo) and Google One Pro (with NotebookLM & Gemini) seriously. He once wandered into Claude Code's "Coreworks" mode, which started to auto-modify PC settings — he refused at the prompt.
  • Core shift: "Generative AI is not 'generative AI' — it is a programming tool, just like RStudio." Don't consume the output; manipulate the computational process with it.
  • Verbal walkthrough of how LLMs work: multi-layer perceptron × Attention = Transformer. Neural nets that the team had abandoned in the 1980s as "useless" turned out to "just work" once layers and parameter counts were scaled up — nobody, not even the inventors, fully explains why. Then someone applied the result to natural language.
  • Attention = a mechanism to bring the relationship between distant words into the weights, which is what unlocked long-document translation.
  • Working pattern: Claude and Gemini run side-by-side, reviewing each other. AI is used as an assistant for slide-ifying / charting one's own writing — not for outsourcing thought.

7.2 Chen et al. (2026) "Out of the Black Box" — uncertainty via logprobes (reported by Matsumae)

  • Pay extra on the ChatGPT API and you can pull logprobes (token log-probabilities). For each output token (e.g. "Yes" / "No") you get a confidence value between 0 and 1.
  • Chen et al. ran hundreds of thousands of news articles through ChatGPT to label each as positive/negative for individual stocks (Yes/No), bucketed the results into 5 confidence tiers, longed the highest-confidence Yes names and shorted the highest-confidence No names. Sharpe ratio > 5–6.
  • Caveat: the paper completely ignores transaction costs and liquidity constraints. The strongest signal sits in small-cap / low-coverage names, so any real-money implementation moves the price against itself. Sharpe will be lower in practice — but the signal direction looks right.
  • Reality check: Claude does not expose logprobes ("we don't ship that," Claude told us itself). Mistral exposes them but quality looks weak. OpenAI is effectively the sole supplier.
  • Asking ChatGPT directly "how confident are you?" (Declared Confidence) makes it lie — Chen verified this. You have to pull the raw data via API.
  • Conclusion: the frontier is moving from "consume the output" to "open the parameters and run empirical research on the network's internals." This is the "AI Expert Scientist" direction.

7.3 The "AI Expert Scientist" concept

  • Kawaguchi's framing: a scientist who wields AI as a programming tool, accesses internals, and runs empirical research with it.
  • Saito-sensei et al. at Waseda built an AI-driven paper-writing tutorial in essentially the same spirit — using AI as a programming tool.
  • This shifts the conversation from philosophy (does AI "understand"?) to empirical practice.

7.4 Independent Thinker vs. Stochastic Parrot

  • Matsumae shared advice from a former PE boss a decade ago: "Become an Independent Thinker. 90% of the world is a stochastic parrot." Chen et al.'s research stance is exactly that: question the surface, dig into the internals.
  • Why / What / How: Why matters most. How (execution) is being driven to zero cost by AI agents.
  • Kawaguchi: Independent Thinkers were never more than ~10% of the population. Their share may shrink further in the AI era — but the value of those who remain skyrockets.
  • Kota Suzuki's challenge: Independent Thinkers don't always win. Riding the top of the stochastic-parrot wave is often more rewarding financially.
  • Kawaguchi quoted Schopenhauer's On Reading: there are two kinds of writers — those who transmit thought, and those who sell text. Today the latter (the stochastic parrot) makes the money.
  • Color: Mori Trust Yaesu Tower aggressively dumped office rents in the Yaesu area (foreign-capital style); Mitsui Fudosan defends "market order" (Japanese style). At Davos 2008 in Dubai, US participants had already pivoted to the next bubble (digital + sustainability) within months of Lehman.

7.5 Reactions from each member

Kohei Iinuma

"AI is a tool only as long as you can verify it. The moment verification becomes impossible, it stops being a tool and starts behaving as if it had intent." Information security (CrowdStrike-class vulnerability discovery) is already entering that unverifiable zone.

Sho Kuroda

  • The statistical idea of "model averaging" suggests we'll soon run Claude / Gemini / Copilot / ChatGPT in parallel and average their answers at the user's end as a standard practice.
  • A deeper question: "Maybe humans, too, only operate on the surface text."
  • When R replaced hand-coded OLS, data analysis didn't get worse — the relevant skill set just shifted. Today's AI looks like that same shape, one level up.
  • Now that non-native English speakers can write fluent papers, paper volume has exploded; reviewers can no longer judge content, so they fall back on author pedigree. Net negative for intellectual reproduction.

Keiichiro Matsumae

In M&A work I run a "poison-bug" loop: Claude Opus writes code, Gemini reviews it, 2–3 turns. Quality goes up. The setup mirrors having two opposing legal counsels argue while the client makes the final call.

Sora Asakawa

Pareto-style: where 20% of knowledge gets you to 100, AI is excellent. At the frontier, AI's output is unreliable. Operate on the assumption that AI only outputs from the past corpus.

Junya Suzuki (tax accountant)

  • Clients are increasingly arriving with ChatGPT-generated "justifications" for things like buying Pokemon cards on the company's account — they cherry-pick prompts to manufacture supportive logic and bring it to the tax accountant.
  • Many laypeople treat AI output as objective truth. Risk of becoming "Wikipedia, but a fictional encyclopedia."
  • Suzuki's own practice: always trace the source links AI cites.

Kota Suzuki

"There's a ceiling on desktop work." Past a certain depth, you only get the "aha" by talking to people. Logprobes have been known in Japan's IT scene since ~2020 — diversifying the people you talk to is a deliberate antidote to "discovering things a year late."

7.6 Programming language vs. natural language — alignment of surface and meaning

  • Kawaguchi: in code, surface = meaning. That's why AI gained traction first in coding (Claude Code). In natural language, surface and meaning diverge (sarcasm being the clearest example), which is exactly the unverifiable zone.
  • Matsumae: cognitive-science distinction constative vs. performative. Constative = surface truth value. Performative = the effect of the utterance on the listener. "Interesting" is a performative property — hard for AI to capture.

7.7 Implication for academia — Kusumegi paper

Kawaguchi cited Kusumegi (Tsukuba): non-native-English researchers got more productive thanks to AI, but aggregate intellectual reproduction at the field level has not improved. Reinforces the stochastic-parrot dynamic where trend-riding papers proliferate.

7.8 The knowledge-search problem

  • Kawaguchi: how do you find the knowledge you actually want? AI is convenient at the entrance but easy to drown in. English Wikipedia covers what you need on NLP / Transformer.
  • Matsumae: real assimilation requires creating an output venue with external pressure. The cognitive mode while talking to AI vs. while producing your own deliverable is fundamentally different.

7.9 Kawaguchi's open question — what is your own value?

"I have a long career and the press still interviews me — but on what basis am I being paid? If I'm just being a stochastic parrot, AI replaces me." This was the closing problem of meeting 2 — homework feeding into meeting 3.

7.10 Can AI make something interesting?

  • Open question raised: can AI pose an interesting research question?
  • Matsumae: it can mimic Murakami's prose, but "interesting" / "a question worth asking" is a different beast. May be a prompt-engineering problem; not yet known.
  • Kuroda: writing language with a target performative effect on the listener seems to remain a human-only skill — at least for now.
